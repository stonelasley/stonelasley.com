## 1. General overview

- The 1675 was introduced circa **1959** as the successor to the reference 6542. [Wind Vintage+3gmtmasterhistory.com+3BQ Watches+3](https://gmtmasterhistory.com/gmt-master_ref_1675.html)
- It remained in production until about **1980** (some sources quote late 1979) making it one of the longest-running sports references from Rolex. [BQ Watches+2gmtmasterhistory.com+2](https://www.bqwatches.com/rolex-gmt-master-1675)
- Key technical features:
    - Stainless steel (also Rolesor and full gold variants) case with crown guards (unlike the 6542). [Andrea Piccinini+2Rolex GMT Master 1675+2](https://www.andreapiccinini.com/en/a-comprehensive-guide-to-the-rolex-gmt-master-1675-with-radial-dial/)
    - Movement: Calibre 1565 in earliest years (~1959-64) then Calibre 1575 thereafter (with hack feature introduced ~1971). [gmtmasterhistory.com+1](https://gmtmasterhistory.com/gmt-master_ref_1675.html)
    - Bezel: Aluminium insert (bi-directional) 24-hour, typical “Pepsi” red/blue, and later black/other colours. [Millenary Watches+1](https://millenarywatches.com/gmt-master-1675/)
    - Crystal: Acrylic (domed). [Wind Vintage+1](https://www.windvintage.com/blog/collectors-guide-the-rolex-gmt-master-reference-1675-in-steel)
- Collectors value the reference for its long production run and many subtle variations (dial, bezel insert, crown guards, bracelet). [BQ Watches+1](https://www.bqwatches.com/rolex-gmt-master-1675)
- Original water resistance: ~50 m/165 ft (not a dive watch). [Rolex GMT Master 1675+1](https://gmtmaster1675.com/the-case/)
- Size: ~38-40 mm diameter, depending on how measured. (Vintage sources often say 38 mm without crown). [Millenary Watches+1](https://millenarywatches.com/gmt-master-1675/)

## 5. Bezel insert variations

The bezel insert on the 1675 is aluminium and has become a major element of character and collector value (fading, colour shifts, rarities). Key variations:

- Standard red/blue “Pepsi” insert used throughout most of the run. [Wind Vintage+1](https://www.windvintage.com/blog/collectors-guide-the-rolex-gmt-master-reference-1675-in-steel)
- Black insert: introduced in early 1970s for some markets. [Millenary Watches](https://millenarywatches.com/gmt-master-1675/)
- “Blueberry” all-blue insert – rarer, especially Middle East/US market. [Millenary Watches](https://millenarywatches.com/gmt-master-1675/)
- Fuchsia/pink-hue faded inserts (red portion fading) found in ~1.8-1.9 m SN range (~1968-69) – very collectible. [Wind Vintage+1](https://www.windvintage.com/rolex-fuchsia-gmtmaster-ref-1675)
- Variant font sizes on insert numerals: “fat-font” inserts seen in early 1970s, smaller fonts earlier. [Wind Vintage+1](https://www.windvintage.com/blog/collectors-guide-the-rolex-gmt-master-reference-1675-in-steel)

| Insert Type              | Colour Code       | Approx. Era        | Font Type                     | Collector Notes                                            |
| ------------------------ | ----------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Early “Pepsi” (Fat Font) | 26-59             | 1959–1970          | Wide serifs and thick numbers | Most valuable; natural fade desired                        |
| “Fuchsia” Fade           | 26-59 (sub-batch) | ~1968 – 69         | Fat Font                      | Red fades to magenta; very collectible                     |
| “Blueberry” (All Blue)   | 26-59B            | ~1970s (ME market) | Medium font                   | Often faked; verify authentic paint and backside engraving |
| “Thin Font Pepsi”        | 26-59A            | ~1971 – 79         | Smaller font                  | Typical later production                                   |
| “Black”                  | 26-60             | 1970s              | Medium font                   | Seen on late 1575 hacking models                           |

**Collector/servicing notes**:

- Original aluminium inserts fade, and such fading is often desirable if natural. Replacement modern inserts exist but change character.
- Ensure the insert is correct to era: e.g., very early gilt examples should have early style insert fonts.
- Avoid aftermarket “fake” inserts claiming rare colour versions unless provenance verified.
- When restoring, if replacing insert, try sourcing genuine vintage correct part (or high quality vintage-spec).

## 7. Authentication & restoration checklist

If you are acquiring or restoring a 1675, here's a consolidated list of what you should check, source, and consider.

**Authentication & originality**

- Verify case serial and model line engraving style and font (see caseback engraving typefaces). [Rolex GMT Master 1675](https://gmtmaster1675.com/the-case/)
- Check crown-guard shape: early pointed crown guards (PCG) vs later rounded crown guards (RCG). Pointed CG must align with early SNs (< 1.1m). [Rolex GMT Master 1675+1](https://gmtmaster1675.com/the-case/)
- Check dial type (Mark variant) is consistent with SN era (gilt vs matte). Incorrect dial is a red flag.
- Hands – verify GMT hand, hour/minute/second hands are correct style for era (e.g., smaller GMT arrow early, larger later). [Watches Guild](https://www.watchesguild.com/articles/rolex-gmt-master-1675)
- Bracelet/bracelet end-links are correct reference for era (7206, 7836, 78360 etc). Check stamped codes and clasp date within ±1 year of watch serial. [Rolex GMT Master 1675+1](https://gmtmaster1675.com/the-bracelet/)
- Crystal: check correct acrylic for era; replacement is acceptable if correct spec but note it might affect value.
- Bezel insert: check wear/fade is plausible and consistent; replacement inserts often reduce originality. Fat fonts end ~1970.
- Movement: verify calibre (1565 vs 1575) and features (hack post ~1971) correspond to era. 1575 hacking only post-1971; pre-1971 non-hack. [Andrea Piccinini](https://www.andreapiccinini.com/en/a-comprehensive-guide-to-the-rolex-gmt-master-1675-with-radial-dial/)
- Lume: Tritium will age cream/yellow; bright white is re-lume.
- Case finishing: Avoid modern high-polish; retain chamfers and original lug bevels.
- Service marks: Inside caseback may have watchmaker codes—normal.
- Documentation: Service invoices and photographic history support provenance.

| Caseback stamp | “1675” inside + year       | Correct font (serif Rolex Geneva)   |

**Restoration & servicing guidance**

- Service interval: Although tool-watch heritage, vintage mechanical watches should be serviced every ~4-7 years depending on use, by competent watchmaker.
- When opening case, inspect movement condition (rust, corrosion) and movement number might help confirm era (though movement numbers not always public).
- Watch the accuracy: the original movement is not a modern chronometer in the sense of super high frequency; reasonable tolerance expected.
- Conservation of case: avoid over-polishing. Collectors value original lug bevels and case dimensions.
- Lume: original tritium lume will age (patina). Acceptable patina is preferred over re-lumed or bright white modern lume unless purpose-built restoration.
- Parts sourcing: For bracelets, crystals, and bezel inserts rely on specialist vintage-Rolex parts houses/re-source networks. Avoid low-cost generic replacements that alter look or fit.
- Replacement crystals: Use correct domed acrylic spec; modern flat or sapphire crystals change the original profile.
- Bezel insert replacement: if original is too damaged, replacing is acceptable but document it (for provenance) and try to source correct era insert (font style / colour).

**Other important notes**

- Paperwork/box: Full set (box, papers, original guarantee) adds value but authenticity of those must also be verified.
- Service history: Knowing prior servicing helps—but for vintage, service casebacks or changed parts are normal; just document changes.
- Polishing history: Case that has been refinished will show thinner lugs/chamfers and reduce collectability.
- Market context: Because 1675 is so popular, there are many “franken” watches (mixed parts), redials, swapped bezels. Buy from reputable dealer or have expert pre-check.
- Restoration philosophy: Decide whether you are restoring for daily wear (then sympathetic upgrading may be OK) or for collector purity (then keep as original as possible).
